## Section 1 — The lineage

> Workler is based on **Jobber**, created by **santifer** and released under the MIT licence.
>
> This project is a fork. It is maintained separately, developed independently, and named
> differently on purpose. Work done here is not upstream's work, and problems here are not
> upstream's problems — bug reports for Workler belong in Workler's repository.

## Section 2 — What the licence covers, and what it does not

*This is the part most attribution pages get wrong, and getting it right is itself a signal.*

> The MIT licence covers code. It does not cover names or logos.
>
> So: Workler uses upstream's code under that licence, and does not use upstream's name, wordmark,
> logo or any other brand asset. Upstream's trademark policy permits describing a derived project
> as "based on Jobber", and that is the only form used here. Workler has never been called Jobber
> and is not a renamed version of it.
>
> The MIT licence text travels with the code and is in the repository.

Claim-with-receipt: link to the LICENSE file in the Workler repository.

## Section 3 — Naming

> "Jobber" is also the trademark of an unrelated field-service software company. Workler is not
> connected to that company either.

## Section 4 — Thanks

> Forking a project is only possible because someone published one. Jobber was released under a
> licence that permits this, which is a choice its author did not have to make.
