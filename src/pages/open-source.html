Read it before you trust it

Workler CLI is the free, MIT-licensed command-line version. It runs inside ten AI coding CLIs on your own machine, and the scoring logic, thirty-three modes and eighteen market vocabulary sets, is readable in the repository. It is a fork of Jobber by santifer, maintained separately under the name Workler.

What it is

The judgment this product sells is a set of instructions a model runs under. You can read all of them.

Thirty-three modes. Eighteen market vocabulary sets. Eighty provider modules for reading public job feeds. The evaluation logic that produces the A–G report and the 1–5 score is not a service call — it is text in a repository, and you can disagree with it in a pull request.

What it does not do

It never submits an application, sends an email, or clicks anything. There is a test that fails if that stops being true.

Free, and the paid version

The command-line version is free and stays free. The hosted version is €79 for ninety days, paid once, and it is paid for by the person using it — no employer, no recruiter, no advertiser.

The boundary is not the judgment. Every mode, the whole scoring, and the posting-legitimacy check are in the free version, under MIT, on your machine. Nothing is held back to make the paid tier look better, and you can check that in the repository rather than taking our word for it.

What the hosted version sells is that you do not have to run it: no AI coding CLI to install, no model keys to hold and pay for separately, no local setup. It adds a chat interface and hosted scanning of the job boards. If you are already comfortable in a coding CLI, the free version is the honest recommendation, and it is the one that never sends your CV anywhere.
